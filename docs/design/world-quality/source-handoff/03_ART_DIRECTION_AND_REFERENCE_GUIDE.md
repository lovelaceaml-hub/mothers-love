## What "higher quality" means here

**Shape:** curved edge profiles that catch light; deliberate thickness; scalloped or beveled architectural details; branches and flower crowns with controlled irregularity. A repeated collection of spheres, cones, cylinders and torus rings may be useful as blockout, not as the final close-up vocabulary.

**Surface:** distinct roughness and reflections for pearl, gold, petals, foliage, water, and silk. Not every object should look like glossy plastic. Fine rim variation and broad reflected light should describe the bubble's curvature; a flat white center surrounded by a thick pink line is insufficient.

**Light:** warm highlights, gentle cool fill, soft but meaningful shading under steps/objects, and enough local contrast to reveal form. Keep the heart pink and the expression colors distinct. Do not use a global pale wash that makes the world gray, or a dark game-like grade to manufacture contrast.

**Space:** every hero shot has a purposeful foreground frame, a clear focal middle distance and a recognizable far landmark. Atmospheric softness increases with distance. The camera should discover shapes at several elevations, not slide between empty circular plates.

**Motion:** calm travel with anticipation and a payoff. Silk turns in and out of the light. Petals open in sequence. New recognition light leads into a formation and then a complete reading moment. More particles or faster panning is not a substitute for choreography.

**Composition:** the user should understand what to look at without operating the TV. A hero bubble or landmark gets clear space. UI and world objects must share a safe layout. A small illustrative neighbor can establish scale but should never become the subject over a recognition.

## Shared palette and finish

The existing catalog remains the source for expression colors and symbols. Reference neutrals are warm ivory/pearl, pale rose, champagne and soft sky blue. Preserve saturated pink/coral, lavender, gold/yellow, blue and fresh green accents. Keep gold delicate: seams, inlays, edge highlights, small emblems, not thick all-screen wirework.

Use image sampling as a calibration aid, not a mandate to assign every material the same screenshot RGB. Color-managed lighting and tone mapping change the final rendered value. Compare runtime frames in the same viewing conditions and record the selected exposure/grade.

## World-wide visual acceptance

At each destination, pause the rendered frame. Inspect silhouette continuity, excessive faceting, intersections, floating contact, repeated props, visible image-card edges, confusing scale and foreground occlusion. If it looks unconvincing when paused, continuous motion will not rescue it.

Then watch the clip in real time. Inspect camera easing, look-at motion, first appearance of text, full reading, reveal/settle sequencing, and effect density. The orbit symbols should look attached to their bubble's spatial system, not like unrelated stickers or text badges drifting across it.

Do not promise exact reproduction of a generated picture under every camera angle and low-power device. Aim for a demonstrably closer art treatment and show the remaining gap. Quality claims must refer to actual output from the committed runtime.

## Things not to take from the pictures

Do not add unapproved expression categories, public sender labels, personal sample records, member-location maps, achievements fabricated from counts, doctrinal text, or additional religious iconography. Preserve the campaign's approved symbols. Do not hard-code the screenshot's wording into a live scene. Do not reproduce the phone chrome in the television output.

The project is a live continuous world. The images are not to become fullscreen background plates, prerecorded replacements for the experience, or alpha-masked cards pretending to be newly modeled architecture.
